# The free toolkit

Every tool the curriculum asks you to use, and what it costs. **237 distinct tools** (from 433
rows across the phase tables), of which **219 are free** and **16 are free-tier or freemium** —
leaving just **2 that are paid outright**.

This is generated by reading the `## Tools for This Phase` tables in `ai-roadmaps/`. The
interactive version is the **Tools** view of the site, which is filterable.

> ⚠️ **Free tiers change.** This page lists what the curriculum says, not what a vendor
> promises today. Where a limit or a term matters to a decision, check the provider's own page.
> The curriculum dates its volatile claims for exactly this reason.

> **These numbers are checked.** `scripts/audit-free-toolkit.mjs` reads the figures on this page
> and fails the build if they disagree with the corpus. The wording of the prose is not
> generated — only the headline counts are enforced, so edit them and the guard will tell you if
> they have gone stale.

## The honest headline

**You do not need to spend anything to complete this curriculum.** Not for the phases, not for
the portfolio pieces, and not for the job hunt. The tools are Python, a free account at one or
two model providers, and things already on your machine.

The paid options that do exist are almost always about **convenience or quota**, not
capability. Where a phase mentions a paid tool it says what it would actually add, and the
answer is usually "nothing you need yet".

## What you actually need to install

| Tool | What it is for | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Python 3** | the language most phases use | Free |
| **VS Code** or **Jupyter** | an editor that runs notebooks | Free |
| **Git** and a **GitHub account** | version control; also your portfolio | Free |
| **`requests`** | calling HTTP APIs | Free |
| A **spreadsheet** | LibreOffice Calc, Google Sheets, or Excel | Free |

That is the whole required list. Everything else in a phase's tools table is either optional,
or is an *activity* rather than software — a stopwatch, a notebook, pen and paper.

## Hosted model access

The curriculum assumes you have access to **at least one** frontier model. You do not need
several.

| Provider | Free path | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Hugging Face Chat** | free | the easiest zero-setup start |
| **OpenRouter** | freemium | one key, many models — good for comparing them |
| **Google AI Studio** | free tier | generous limits for learning |
| **OpenAI** | paid, no monthly fee | pay-per-token; a few dollars lasts a long time |
| **Anthropic** | paid, no monthly fee | same |

The curriculum also treats **a provider's own pricing page, rate-limit page and data-usage
policy** as tools worth reading deliberately. Those three pages answer most questions about
what a system will actually cost and what happens to your data.

⚠️ **Free tiers often train on your data.** That is a real trade, not a footnote. Never put
anything confidential, personal, or under an NDA into a free tier that trains on inputs.

## Coding assistants

The curriculum's position: **the assistant is not the skill.** Use one, but every phase asks
you to verify what it produced. Learning to catch its mistakes is the actual objective.

Several phases deliberately have you work with **any chat assistant you already have**, rather
than a specific product, so the technique does not expire with a vendor's free tier.

## Fine-tuning

| Tool | Notes | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **peft** | Hugging Face's parameter-efficient fine-tuning library (LoRA and friends) | Free/open-source |
| **trl** | training loops for fine-tuning and preference tuning | Free/open-source |
| **bitsandbytes** | quantization, which is what makes small-GPU fine-tuning possible | Free/open-source |
| **Unsloth** | faster, lower-memory fine-tuning | Free/open-source |
| **Kaggle** / **Colab** | free GPU for small runs | Free tier |

⚠️ **Fine-tuning is the most expensive thing in this curriculum to run for real.** The phases
are written so you can complete them on a small model, or on a free notebook GPU, and reason
about the results rather than needing a large run. LoRA and QLoRA are the **techniques**; `peft`
is the library that implements them.

## Things that are not software

Some entries are not tools at all — they are **things you already have**, listed because a phase
asks you to use them:

- a plain text or Markdown file
- a spreadsheet
- a notebook
- a stopwatch
- a recording app on your phone
- the job posts you have already collected
- your own `portfolio/` folder in this repository

They appear in the tools tables because a practice task genuinely calls for them, and because
the alternative — an empty cell — reads as an omission rather than a deliberate choice.

**No count is given here, on purpose.** An earlier version of this page claimed "31 of the 229
entries are not tools at all" above a list of seven. The number was never derived from anything:
the corpus has no field that marks an entry as "not software", so any total would be a hand
tally dressed up as data. The list above is the honest form of the claim — it says what the
category contains without inventing a figure for it.

## How to read the cost column

| Label | Means |
|---|---|
| **Free** | no payment, no card, no time limit that matters |
| **Free/open-source** | free, and you can read and modify the source |
| **Free tier** | a real free allowance with paid limits above it |
| **Freemium** | usable free; the useful parts may be paid |
| **Paid** | requires payment |

**"Free tier" is not the same as "free".** A free tier can change, expire, or train on your
data. That distinction is why the curriculum prefers tools that are free outright.
